What the map shows

  • Six of the twelve functions that GVSB1.0 lists as minimal capacity have one curriculum section or none. Build-up systems need these first.
  • Three minimal-capacity functions make up passive surveillance standardization: reporting (IV.1A), investigation (IV.2A) and causality assessment (IV.3A). The Smart PV strategy places all three in the short term for systems below maturity level 3 (Table 2, p. 22). This is proposed as the first roadmap task.
  • AI applications in curriculum 18.3 attach only to the front of the data flow: intake, coding, data quality, causality and signals. None attach to decisions or communication.
  • In the WHO example plans for a new vaccine (Smart PV strategy, Tables 2 and 3, pp. 22–23), no action reaches immunization policy, and only one reaches regulatory decisions based on vigilance information (GBT VL05.01), as a secondary link. The step where surveillance results become decisions is nearly empty in the examples.
  • Immunization policy is the only function with no source ID. It is grounded in GVSB2.0 p. 1, II.3 (p. 13) and V.3 (p. 27).

How a section is assigned. Each of the 107 curriculum sections was assigned by five ordered questions: is it an activity of the system, knowledge needed for a function, adjacent to pharmacovigilance at a defined interface, an AI method, or none of these. 25 sections are knowledge, 23 out of scope and 4 PV-adjacent; they are not shown here. “Thin (candidate)” means exactly one section. Section count is a screening rule only: whether that one section covers what the function requires will be checked against the source documents and in SIG review.
